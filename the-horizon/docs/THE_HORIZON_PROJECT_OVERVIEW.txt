THE HORIZON — COMPLETE ROBLOX GAME PROJECT OVERVIEW

1. GENERAL GAME DESCRIPTION

Game Name: The Horizon

Platform: Roblox

Genre: Open-world multiplayer survival, exploration, crafting, and base building.

Inspiration: Rust, ARK: Survival Evolved, and other open-world survival games.

Character Type: Roblox R6

Camera: Primarily first-person gameplay.

Supported Devices: PC and mobile.

Development Status: The main UI systems have largely been designed or developed. Several are nearly finished, while the main survival world, models, gameplay mechanics, and final integrations still need work.

What is The Horizon?

The Horizon is an ambitious Roblox survival game where players create their own worlds, explore enormous islands, gather resources, hunt wildlife, craft tools and weapons, build shelters, and survive dangerous environments.

The focus is on exploration, survival, and freedom.

Players are not forced to follow one linear storyline. Instead, they can decide how they want to survive, where they want to settle, which islands to explore, what resources to gather, and what structures to build.

The game is designed primarily around solo survival and cooperative multiplayer with friends.

It combines realistic survival concepts with Roblox-friendly, stylized graphics and clean, modern interfaces.

The long-term goal is to create a large, immersive, polished survival experience that feels like an actual standalone survival game rather than a typical Roblox simulator.

2. HOW THE GAME WORKS — FULL GAMEPLAY LOOP

Step 1 — Entering the Game

When players first launch The Horizon, they enter the main lobby rather than immediately spawning into the survival map.

The lobby acts as the central hub for managing survival worlds and accessing different game features.

Players can:

* Play the game.
* Create a new survival world.
* Open their existing saved worlds.
* Join friends who are playing.
* Join worlds using codes.
* Manage their available world slots.
* Access support and donation options.
* Submit bug reports and suggestions.
* Access other lobby interfaces.

Step 2 — Creating a World

Players can create their own survival world.

The world creation interface is designed to handle information such as the world’s name and difficulty.

Each world has its own identity and should maintain independent survival progress.

World slots determine how many separate worlds a player can own.

Additional slots can be obtained using optional purchases.

The goal is to allow players to return to their worlds without losing their survival progression, structures, or collected resources.

Important: The existing lobby implementation includes world records and metadata saving. Complete gameplay-world persistence and loading have not yet been verified as finished.

Step 3 — Starting Survival

Once the player enters a survival world, the actual gameplay begins.

The intended survival loop is:

1. Explore the starting environment.
2. Find basic resources.
3. Gather stones, leaves, wood, and other materials.
4. Craft simple tools.
5. Collect food and water.
6. Maintain health, hunger, and thirst.
7. Hunt wildlife and defend against hostile creatures.
8. Build a shelter or permanent base.
9. Create improved equipment.
10. Explore other islands for resources, discoveries, and challenges.
11. Continue developing the player’s survival world.

The game is intended to support long-term survival progression rather than short, repetitive rounds.

3. THE OPEN WORLD AND ISLAND SYSTEM

The Horizon takes place across a huge interconnected ocean environment.

The planned map contains approximately 30 islands.

Earlier planning considered overall world dimensions in the tens of thousands of studs, including approximately 50,000–70,000 studs. The exact final dimensions have not been locked.

The islands are intended to be separated by large stretches of water, with approximately 2,000–3,000 studs between some islands.

Planned Biomes

Grasslands

* Open grassy landscapes.
* Natural areas for exploration and settlements.
* Forests, stones, and basic resources.

Jungles

* Dense vegetation.
* Tropical environments.
* Wildlife and natural resources.

Savannahs

* Dry, warm landscapes.
* Open terrain with scattered vegetation.
* Distinct wildlife and environments.

Swamps

* Wet, muddy environments.
* Shallow water and dense vegetation.
* More difficult terrain to explore.

Antarctica / Frozen Regions

* Snow and ice.
* Extremely cold-looking environments.
* Unique terrain and potential environmental survival challenges.

Not every island should look or feel the same. Islands should have their own natural geography, vegetation, resources, and points of interest.

Monuments and Exploration Locations

The game also has planned monuments and special locations.

Previously discussed monument scope:

* 5 large, rare monuments.
* 3 medium monuments.
* 2 small monuments.

Examples of suitable locations include ruined castles and abandoned camps.

The world should maintain a survival-oriented, natural atmosphere rather than depending on modern cities or futuristic buildings.

These monuments are planned exploration content, not confirmed completed map features.

5. SURVIVAL SYSTEM

The main survival mechanics revolve around maintaining the player’s basic needs.

Health

Health represents the player’s physical condition.

Damage from enemies and survival hazards can reduce health.

When health reaches zero, the player dies and enters the death interface.

Hunger

Hunger is a survival stat that requires the player to obtain and consume food.

Food sources are intended to include gathered and hunted resources.

Thirst

Thirst requires the player to obtain drinkable water.

Water-related items include:

* Seawater Cup.
* Clean Water Cup.
* Plastic Cup.

The resource and drinking systems are part of the intended survival gameplay.

Temperature

Temperature and environmental conditions were also considered as survival mechanics.

However, the temperature icons were removed from the latest HUD design.

Temperature should not be assumed to be a fully implemented mechanic.

HUD

The main HUD has already been designed.

It displays health, hunger, and thirst.

The style is relatively minimal, with readable values and visual status bars.

Design characteristics include:

* Semi-transparent backgrounds.
* Subtle visual texture.
* Clean icons.
* Simple text.
* Minimal unnecessary outlines.
* An uncluttered first-person experience.

The health, hunger, and thirst HUD designs were reported finished.

The server-side survival logic still needs to be confirmed separately from the interface.

9. BUILDING SYSTEM

The Horizon has a separate building system that uses an equippable Building Hammer.

This system is different from ordinary inventory crafting.

Building Hammer

When a player equips the Building Hammer, the building controls become available.

Players should be able to:

* Open the building menu.
* Choose a structure or placeable.
* Preview where it will be placed.
* Rotate the object.
* Confirm placement.
* Construct survival buildings.

Building Materials

The building interface is intended to support structural categories and different material variants, including wood and stone.

The UI was designed with a central preview and simple item navigation.

Planned Building Pieces

The system is intended to support house construction, including structural pieces such as walls and other building components.

The exact finalized collection of building pieces has not been fully confirmed.

Separate Placeable Objects

The following objects were moved out of the ordinary crafting interface and into the Building Hammer system:

* Campfire.
* Fire starter.
* Sleeping mat.
* Fish trap.
* Small raft.

The goal is to make placement feel like a construction activity rather than simply receiving a finished Tool from crafting.

PC and Mobile Controls

PC players use keyboard and mouse controls.

Mobile players receive dedicated touch buttons for opening the building menu and rotating structures.

The controls should be compact and should not obstruct the middle of the screen unnecessarily.

A placement prompt can display actions such as Place Wall.

The building UI design has been extensively discussed, but complete server-authoritative building placement and structure saving have not been verified as finished.

18. MONETIZATION

The Horizon is not intended to depend heavily on pay-to-win monetization.

The approved monetization direction focuses on world management, creative gameplay, support purchases, and convenience features that do not unfairly affect survival progression.

Approved Purchases

+1 World Slot

Allows the player to own one additional survival world.

+3 World Slots

Adds three available world slots.

Unlimited World Slots

Removes the regular world-slot ownership limit.

Creative World Pass

Unlocks the creative-world functionality and its associated creative tools through one pass.

Creative tools should not require separate purchases.

Animal Tracker

A previously approved convenience feature priced at 79 Robux.

Support Donations

Optional purchases for players who want to support development.

Gifting

Purchases such as world passes are intended to be giftable.

Rejected Monetization

The game should avoid:

* Paid inventory expansions.
* Paid access to the normal map.
* Monetized world expansions.
* Blueprint-slot purchases.
* Archived-world slot purchases.
* Shared-world-ownership purchases.
* Advanced map marker purchases.
* Unnecessary decoration-pack monetization.
* Combat advantages sold through Robux.

The goal is to monetize in ways that do not undermine the survival experience.

21. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE AND SECURITY

The Horizon uses Roblox Luau.

The project includes LocalScripts, server Scripts, and ModuleScripts.

Some systems have separate client and server components.

Important Technical Requirements

* Support both PC and mobile.
* Preserve R6 compatibility.
* Use server-authoritative inventory and item grants.
* Validate crafting recipes and material ownership on the server.
* Validate combat and damage on the server.
* Validate building placement on the server.
* Keep world ownership and progress isolated.
* Handle DataStore operations safely.
* Avoid duplication bugs.
* Avoid unnecessary performance-heavy loops.
* Preserve existing Roblox object names and hierarchy when updating scripts.

Previous Script Recovery Findings

An October 2026 recovery of archived scripts identified several possible integration problems:

1. Some death/respawn scripts used mismatched RemoteEvent names.
2. The recovered inventory client lacked matching authoritative server handlers.
3. Crafting depended on a server-side world identifier that was not being initialized by the recovered world server.
4. Some survival and sprint scripts handled only part of their intended mechanics.
5. Some objective scripts contained demonstration behavior.
6. Multiple scripts could conflict if installed simultaneously.
7. Developer-product receipt handling needed to avoid competing callbacks.

These findings are from older saved script packages, not a fresh verification of the current Roblox Studio project.

Any developer or AI working on the project should inspect the actual current scripts before deciding which issues remain.

22. OVERALL GAME VISION

The Horizon should ultimately feel like a complete multiplayer survival adventure.

Players should have the freedom to create a world, explore its islands, develop a home, gather resources, craft equipment, survive the environment, encounter wildlife and enemies, and progressively become better equipped.

Exploration should feel rewarding.

Islands should be meaningfully different from each other.

Resource gathering should feed into crafting and building.

Building should let players create their own survival settlements.

Combat should be responsive and varied.

Playing with friends should feel like surviving together in a persistent world.

The game should maintain its own recognizable identity while taking inspiration from established survival games.

The objective is quality, immersion, replayability, and long-term progression.

23. IMPORTANT INSTRUCTIONS FOR ANY AI OR DEVELOPER WORKING ON THIS PROJECT

This is an existing game project, not a new project being started from scratch.

Do not unnecessarily recreate systems that have already been made.

Do not assume every discussed feature is implemented.

Always distinguish:

* Features already completed.
* Features visually designed.
* Features partially scripted.
* Features planned but not implemented.
* Features requiring testing or integration.

Preserve existing functionality unless explicitly asked to change it.

Do not rename objects, change the project’s architecture, or replace working code without a reason.

Do not introduce new gameplay systems, monetization features, recipes, or major design changes without approval.

For scripting work, inspect the current source before making modifications.

The final goal is to combine all existing development work into a complete, functioning, optimized, and immersive Roblox survival game called THE HORIZON.
